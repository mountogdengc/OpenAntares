# Research Note: Faction Rosters in Space Strategy Games

A survey of how four space strategy series build their playable peoples: *Master of Orion*, *Sins of a Solar Empire*, *Homeworld*, and *StarDrive*. It was compiled from a conversational summary, not from manuals or primary sources. Treat names and details as approximate, and check a source before quoting any of them. The September 2026 *Sins II* expansion and the 2026 *Homeworld 3* DLC in particular are recent and unverified.

This is a study of design patterns, not a source of names, species, or lore. Per the [charter](../CHARTER.md) and [LICENSING.md](../../LICENSING.md), nothing here should be copied into OpenAntares. The [species roster](../SPECIES_ROSTER.md) was written independently and stays the authority for our peoples.

## 1. Master of Orion: a large roster of archetypes with bonuses

| Game | Playable roster | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Master of Orion* (1993) | Ten races | Each race is one animal or concept with one strength: bird pilots, bear-like ground troops, shapeshifting spies, cat gunners, big-brained researchers, fast-breeding reptiles, an insect hive built for production, cyborgs, rock life that ignores pollution, and diplomatic humans. A guardian ship defends the non-playable Orions. |
| *Master of Orion II* (1996) | Thirteen, plus custom races | Adds telepaths, lucky financiers, and aquatic navigators. Players can build a custom race from traits. Non-playable invaders arrive in the endgame. |
| *Master of Orion III* (2003) | Sixteen | Races are grouped into body families (humanoid, cybernetic, saurian, aquatic, ethereal, geodic, insectoid, harvester). Several classic races become minor "magnate" factions instead of starting empires. |
| *Master of Orion: Conquer the Stars* (2016) | The classic ten, later thirteen | Brings back the original roster, with one-planet independent minor civilizations and custom races. |

**Pattern.** A race is an animal archetype paired with a stat bonus. The roster is easy to read, but races mostly differ by percentages on the same economy. The custom builder in the second game shows players wanted to mix traits more than they wanted any particular race.

## 2. Sins of a Solar Empire: few factions, deep differences

| Game | Playable sides | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original (2008) | Three | A human trader coalition (armor, missiles, strong economy), human psychic exiles (shields, energy weapons, culture as a weapon), and alien refugees fleeing an unseen enemy (phase travel, stripping planets for resources). Pirates are a non-playable force that can be bribed. |
| *Rebellion* (2012) | Six | Each faction splits in two, roughly "hold and build" against "attack and move". Each side gets its own capital titan ship. |
| *Sins of a Solar Empire II* | Six, later seven | The same splits, renamed. The expansion makes the refugees' pursuer playable. It is built on corruption that trades defense for offense, and weapons that ignore shields. |

**Pattern.** Only one of the three founding factions is non-human, yet each plays very differently because each has its own economy and its own way of moving. Splitting a faction later adds variety without inventing new peoples. The threat the story keeps offscreen becomes a playable side later.

## 3. Homeworld: a story with peoples in it, not a pick list

The series is driven by its campaign, so its peoples are story roles: exiles returning home, an old empire that exiled them, ancient neutral traders, mercenary pirates, a parasite that converts ships, nomadic crusaders, a long-dead precursor race whose gates drive the plot, and a newer enemy inside a hyperspace anomaly. Most are human or humanoid. The prequel splits one people into rival clans on their home world.

**Pattern.** Differences come from history, culture, and doctrine more than biology. "Clans of one people" adds conflict without adding species. Neutral traders and dead precursors give the setting depth without needing their own playable balance.

## 4. StarDrive: backer-designed races with odd bodies

| Game | Playable roster | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *StarDrive* (2013) | Eight | Five came from Kickstarter backer prompts. They include immobile molluscs who rule through mind-control pheromones, reptile liars who show a hologram during diplomacy, bear-like honor warriors, a machine hive, mobile pacifist plants, religious cephalopods, pack-hunting canines with fast but weak ships, and balanced humans. Pirates and ancient remnant ships are non-player hazards. |
| *StarDrive 2* (2015) | Nine | Adds mercantile insect slavers. The molluscs' workforce is a second species from the same homeworld that does not start as an empire. A waking ancient power is the late-game threat. |

**Pattern.** The memorable races have a body or social rule that changes how you play them: an immobile ruler with a servant species, or plants that grow fast and want peace. The forgettable ones are another animal plus a combat bonus.

## 5. Lessons for OpenAntares

- **A structural limit beats a percentage.** Across all four series the factions players remember change what you can do, not how much. This supports the [species roster](../SPECIES_ROSTER.md) choice of one structural limit per people.
- **Avoid the animal-archetype checklist.** Bird, bear, cat, insect, reptile and rock races are a shared genre shorthand. Reusing it makes a game read as a clone even when no names are copied.
- **A small roster can be enough.** *Sins* gets years of variety from three founding factions. Four peoples is a reasonable start if each has a different job.
- **Splits and clans are cheap depth.** Splitting one people into factions with different doctrines adds variety later without new biology. This fits the roster's choice to keep culture separable from body.
- **Non-playable presences give the setting scale.** Pirates, neutral traders, minor one-planet civilizations, precursors, and an endgame threat appear in almost every series. They are later-milestone content. The charter's first playable milestone needs none of them.
- **Custom builders come from wanting to mix traits.** If OpenAntares ever adds a custom setup, it should pair the structural limits that already exist, not a list of stat bonuses.
