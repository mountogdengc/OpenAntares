## Intent, authority and starting point

Make the choice between cooperation and coercion matter. A player should be able to defend an expanding civilization, threaten a rival's industry, lose valuable ships, negotiate an end to fighting and recover. War must have a limited purpose even before conquest exists. A production blockade supplies that purpose without introducing population casualties or changing colony ownership.

Authorities: [charter](../../CHARTER.md), [engineering rules](../../../CLAUDE.md), [workflow](../../PROJECT_WORKFLOW.md), the approved fleet-command, combat, ship-design and diplomacy specifications, and [diplomacy integration](../../../reports/DIPLOMACY_INTEGRATION_2026-10-31.md). The integration report records 2,140 tests and 856 native Diplomacy checks; these are historical implementation results, not tests of this proposal.

Existing rivals expand peacefully. Existing combat attacks neutral sentries; a move never starts a battle. Damage, cohesion, four doctrines, withdrawal, officer loss/recovery, repair, paid equipment designs and private production trade already exist. Preserve their mechanics unless an ECF contract explicitly extends them for new rules. All choices below, including numeric defaults, are drafter proposals for full review, not measured balance.

## Scope and alternatives

Recommended first slice: bilateral war/peace, deliberately initiated fleet battles, defensive withdrawal, paid rival warship construction, and finite production blockades. Keep civilians protected. Ships and officers can be lost; colonies and populations cannot be attacked or captured.

| Approach | Benefit | Limit | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| War declarations and fleet battles alone | Smallest military connection | Fighting has little leverage over a rival that avoids deploying ships | Add a bounded blockade as the strategic stake |
| Limited war with production blockades | Connects diplomacy, economy, equipment, fleet orders and repair | Colonies remain permanently owned; war cannot eliminate an empire | Recommended milestone |
| Full conquest and tactical battles | Larger military game | Needs civilian targeting, invasion, occupation, defeat and a second battle interface | Separate later designs |

No bombardment, invasion, occupation, colony damage/destruction, civilian attacks, capture, elimination/victory condition, surrender terms, tribute, reparations, alliances, coalition battles, border closure, convoy logistics, fuel/upkeep currency, new hulls/equipment, espionage, war exhaustion, species combat modifiers or tactical screen. Peaceful mode [FR-001](../../FEATURE_REQUESTS.md#fr-001-peaceful-mode) remains a separate deferred request. Existing Solo, rival, sentry and ameba settings remain independent. New campaigns explain that rivals can declare limited war; this is not delivered as a new peaceful-mode toggle.

## ECF-001 Relations and declaration

Each contacted unordered empire pair has Peace or War, a monotonic conflict revision and dated current war/last peace facts. New pairs begin at Peace. A pair that has never met cannot be named in a war command. Radio contact grants no location, economic or military intelligence.

Declare war is an explicit command available only at Peace, outside an enforced truce, with neither party already in a different war. **One active opponent per empire** is a first-slice gameplay limit; independent wars between disjoint pairs may coexist. There are no calls to allies or third-party penalties. Show the limit before confirmation without disclosing an unobserved third party. A command whose availability depends on newly changed private state returns a generic unavailable result, not another opponent's identity.

The declaration is a queued intent. It is public only when its boundary resolves; it cannot authorize an attack in the same planning turn. The proposer may revoke its own pending declaration before Ready/resolution. Ordinary Ready/unready and stale-command rules apply. Crossed declarations on the same pair create one war, retaining both declarers. Competing declarations involving a shared empire resolve by canonical pair key (lower EmpireId, higher EmpireId); unsuccessful intents produce a private generic unavailable result, no war or penalty. Never select by wall-clock or command arrival order.

Example: declaration in planning T resolves T->T+1. T->T+1 has no empire battle/blockade from the declaration. War is published at T+1; either side can then issue an attack or blockade. A queued declaration cannot silently redirect a fleet or expose an opponent's pending orders. Its confirmation names the opponent, explains treaty losses and protected civilians, and states the effective date. On publication both parties receive persistent Attention with Diplomacy and fleet links. A war declaration itself does not require a modal response to Ready.

## ECF-002 Agreements, attitude and wartime diplomacy

At war publication, every settlement accord and trade agreement between the pair ends, even inside its minimum duration or notice. This is a deliberate new-rules early-breach rule. Neither setup payment is refunded. No unrelated agreement or third empire is affected.

Resolve valid declaration intents before deciding outcomes of pending ordinary offers. Cancel the pair's outstanding settlement/trade offer and any queued answer, release all its own setup earmarks before that boundary's economy, and charge neither side a new setup fee. Withdrawal/revoke already accepted remains effective. A previously live agreement still supplies its frozen T->T+1 effects; it ends in the T+1 publication. Thus a declaration does not retroactively stop that last trade installment or undo that boundary's settlement restriction. After publication no further installment/founding restriction survives. UI previews distinguish this last effective interval from an immediate reservation release.

While War is active, ordinary settlement/trade offers and normal agreement termination commands are unavailable; peace is the only negotiation. No cooperation streak accumulates. Both directed streaks reset at war publication. Existing cancellation memory ages normally. Record a separate declaration grievance against each declaring empire: penalty30 for the counterpart during the war and for20 completed peaceful boundaries after peace. Crossed declarations give each party the grievance. Repeated declarations refresh this memory rather than stack it. War-caused treaty endings do not add ordinary cancellation penalties; a pre-existing cancellation memory remains until its own expiry. New-rules opinion is `2*cooperationStreak - cancellationMemory - declarationGrievance`, core range -40..20. Show every term and the post-peace expiration date; while at War label relations War rather than suggesting a negative score alone defines hostility. Unknown content variants use their validated magnitudes.

Ending a war never restores agreements, setup payments, cooperation streaks or an older founding block. Peacefully rebuilding trust requires fresh ordinary offers and payments under their usual rules. Third-party commerce remains private and valid.

## ECF-003 Peace offers and recovery truce

Either belligerent can offer unconditional peace at any time; no minimum war length and no economic price. Use one pending offer per pair, three planning-boundary lifetime, accept/decline/withdraw/revoke, exact own queued answer and the existing answer-before-expiry/withdraw-beats-answer semantics. There are no territorial terms or automatic surrender. A recipient may accept at any attitude; human acceptance is always explicit. Sending peace while an incoming peace offer exists means queuing Accept on that existing offer; show this before submission. If two new proposals are generated from the same unanswered snapshot, the lower EmpireId becomes the canonical proposer and the other becomes its queued accepting respondent, creating one offer rather than two. The canonical proposer can Withdraw, cancelling the whole offer and beating the answer; the respondent can Revoke its answer, leaving the original incoming offer unanswered. There is no second outgoing offer to resurrect. Simultaneous human command submission normalizes to these same roles; receipt order cannot change who is canonical or whether a party has revoked its consent. AI-issued offers must leave a real published answer window, including alternate lifetime1 content.

Successful peace resolves **before economy and empire combat for that boundary**. Suppress all bilateral blockade reductions and all bilateral pending/active battle volleys, interceptions and new attack activations on that boundary; neither side gets a final free shot. Close their encounters, preserve damage/cohesion and clear finite Attack/Blockade/withdrawal orders into Hold at the current system. A ship already in interstellar transit completes its existing ordinary journey; a pre-existing ordinary Move/Return outside an encounter is preserved. Peace does not repair ships, repatriate fleets or modify sentry combat. Reports state that no final exchange occurred.

Peace published at T+1 enforces **10 complete peaceful boundaries**. New declarations become legal in planning T+11, after T+1->T+2 through T+10->T+11. Pending war intents cannot bypass this truce. The truce cannot be breached or paid away. Trade/settlement offers may resume during it. Declines and expiries have no attitude cost. Persist exact offer and truce dates using checked wide calculations near the supported turn limit.

## ECF-004 Legal military targets and visibility

Only armed empire vessels (effective base design attack >0) are wartime targets. Scouts, settlement vessels, passengers, colonies, population, buildings and officers aboard protected civilian flagships cannot be targeted directly. Pure civilian fleets remain safe to move, survey and found under existing ameba, sentry, quarantine and settlement rules. A civilian presence cannot prevent a blockade. This protection is a transparent first-slice restriction, not an assertion about later conquest rules.

Attack requires a stationary own named fleet containing only armed vessels, 1..32 members, currently at the same star as a currently observed hostile armed vessel. It cannot be in transit, newly completed on this boundary, refitting/repairing, in active sentry combat, in another empire encounter, or committed to assembly/founding/ameba activity. An incoming enemy force cannot be attacked remotely or while in transit. Arrival may reveal a target; it never fires on that same boundary. Ordinary movement and Hold do not authorize an attack; automatic counterfire in a battle is defensive, not a global attack-on-sight order.

Select a currently observed hostile **anchor VesselId**, not a hidden fleet-order token. At resolution its actual stationary armed group is the defender: the armed members of its current named fleet, or that single ship if ungrouped. Splitting, renaming or creating a fleet cannot make an otherwise still-present anchor untargetable. A different anchor in the same group still refers to that same defending force for conflict matching. Targeting a ship that has legitimately gone into transit before the planning snapshot is unavailable. If the anchor is lost before combat, cancel the attack without substituting a different target; show no private loss cause.

Own current presence, including an owned colony, observes foreign stationary armed hulls in that star. Extend legitimate local observations to include those hulls' public equipment statistics, current hull band/points and fleet grouping sufficient for an encounter preview. Do not disclose reserve, queues, technologies not embodied in observed equipment, admiral identity/rank, cohesion, return port, doctrine, hidden movement intent or another system. No global military-strength number. Current foreign movement markers still do not reveal transit routes. After presence is lost keep dated last-observed facts, explicitly stale; stale facts can guide ordinary travel but cannot authorize an attack/blockade or exact remote damage forecast.

Participants learn the actual opposing armed hulls and applied shot/damage calculations in their encounter. Opposing cohesion, standing doctrine and withdrawal destination remain private even after a fight; an actual departure is observed, not its hidden route. A third-party observer sees only the normal public local hull facts and disappearance, never private battle reports or diplomatic relations. Foreign fleet names and officers are not required for targeting; use original neutral labels such as observed force at the named system.

## ECF-005 Encounter matching and fixed forces

Attack intents reserve only the issuing force; they do not mutate a foreign force, publish a warning or increment a foreign revision during planning. Match all new intents from the frozen boundary snapshot, after peaceful outcomes but before new empire volleys. Sort by StarId, canonical war-pair key, then attacking fleet's lowest participating VesselId. A ship/force can enter at most one encounter. Crossed attacks between the same two forces collapse into one encounter; duplicates and losing competing attacks return the unused own force to Hold with a bounded target-unavailable result. No submission-order advantage, extra volley or leak through a preview token. Previously active encounters keep their existing participants before new matching.

Freeze each side's armed participants at first activation, max32 per side. No reinforcement, repair, refit, regrouping or officer transfer during a live encounter. Fleet identity, civilian membership and orders outside the armed battle subset are preserved. A mixed defending fleet's armed ships participate; its civilians cannot take damage. Ordinary group travel then uses the departure rules below for the armed subset, while keeping civilians with their fleet. An officer contributes to combat escape only if its designated flagship participates; a protected civilian flagship supplies no combat Navigator bonus. Existing flagship-loss/recovery mechanics apply to participating armed flagships only.

At most64 active empire encounters per empire; unused attack intents fail privately if this bound is reached. No active participant can also fight a sentry. A force already in an active sentry encounter is temporarily unavailable as an empire target and cannot blockade; its presence still contests a blockade. This is an explicit bounded staging restriction, not invulnerability for other ships in the star. A newly pending sentry attack loses priority to an incoming eligible empire attack: cancel it, retain all ships and report the superseding battle. Active sentry encounters otherwise retain their old rules. Additional fleets may wait, travel or target another uncommitted hostile force; there is no allied damage pooling or multi-sided encounter.

## ECF-006 Exchanges, orders and automatic defense

Use M9/M10 equipment, damage bands, effective firepower, armor, minimum1 damage per armed shot, simultaneous frozen targeting and persistent hull/cohesion. Both sides are now empire ships. Every living armed participant fires once per ordinary boundary, targeting the lowest opposing VesselId; excess damage does not spill and ships destroyed in the exchange still fire. Both sides suffer the existing cohort-local cohesion losses for destroyed/newly crippled ships and participating flagship loss. Mutual destruction closes the battle without survivors or territorial change. No loot, free equipment, XP from kills or combat RNG.

Reuse Cautious (initial), Press the attack, Hit and run and explicit Hold despite losses. Their M9 retreat thresholds remain exact. Each fleet stores defensive doctrine and retreat preference, separately from whether it has issued an attack. Ungrouped armed ships have Cautious and local Break contact initially. A human can configure these through previews before hostilities. War warning text explains that deployed armed ships may be attacked and defend themselves even without an Attack order; protected civilians are never opted into risk by this warning.

First enemy activation performs one ordinary exchange unless either side already has a legal departing/withdrawal intent. A defending force does not need a fresh consent checkbox to return fire. After an ordinary volley the doctrine may queue withdrawal for the next boundary. Same-turn changes apply from the accepted own planning intent; they never rewrite a completed volley. Hold despite losses remains explicit human opt-in; AI never selects it.

Health/survival loss before the volley invalidates the affected participant revision. Cancel a pending first encounter without shots; for an active encounter skip both sides' shots that boundary, normalize actual losses and expose the owner's revised-force response. The uninjured opponent gets only “encounter paused: force changed,” with its own observations. Each human must review its own revised force before Ready or choose withdrawal; no repeated pause arises merely from trimming a report. Existing plague, ameba and officer recovery streams/order remain authoritative. Repeated loss can cost a boundary but cannot reset hull, cohesion or produce extra shots.

## ECF-007 Departure, withdrawal and aftermath

An attacker confirms its doctrine, an explicit eligible own return-colony system other than the battle star, Keep together / Detach crippled stragglers, and On arrival Repair / Hold. Core selections remain Cautious / Keep together / Repair. A defending fleet uses its saved defensive settings; if it has no valid return authorization, its fallback is **local Break contact**, not a silently selected home. UI explains this default when warships are created/grouped and on declaration. An accepted ordinary Move/Return by a newly attacked defender counts as its authorized departing route, not as immunity to being shot.

For unilateral departure, compare each departing cohort's current slowest effective speed with the fastest surviving opposing armed participant's current effective speed, frozen before interception. Strictly faster cohorts escape without damage. Slower/equal cohorts receive one enemy-only interception volley, then surviving ships depart at their post-volley speed. Departing ships do not fire. The opponent never also fires an ordinary volley that boundary. Detachment and flagship custody follow M9; civilians in a defending mixed fleet stay with its main cohort, contribute to its travel speed and never become targets. Ships/cargo/flagships lost and recovered retain existing finite aftermath behavior.

If **both sides depart**, both leave without a volley, irrespective of speed. If neither departs, resolve exactly one ordinary exchange. If all armed participants on a side die, its protected civilians remain safe and the encounter ends; do not continue shooting them. Survivors on the other side Hold, with their current damage/cohesion. No winner auto-blockades or pursues into transit.

Local Break contact resolves the same exposure comparison/interception as unilateral departure but makes no interstellar journey; close the encounter and leave survivors in Hold at the star. It grants no shelter, immunity, control of the system or immunity from a fresh attack next planning turn. It is a release from the fixed encounter, not a guaranteed escape. Both local breaks produce no shots. If an authorized port becomes illegal/missing, substitute this disclosed local fallback and persistent Attention; do not stall Ready indefinitely, teleport or invent a new port. A newly failed local break can expose the ships again to a later independently initiated encounter.

Existing arrival/repair clocks, damage-based fleet travel, refit eligibility and officer/cargo rules stay unchanged. No arrival repair tick; never repair and fire on one boundary. A combatant's repair intent must end its battle/blockade first. During a blockade, its eligible defenders cannot repair while actively engaged; stored production is still available to pay an otherwise legal replacement/refit before engagement. Refitting armed hulls are not immune targets. Before their first defensive volley, cancel the pending refit job, clear its occupied port slot with construction automation left paused, preserve its unstarted queue and bank, retain the currently installed design, and make no completion payment. M10 charges once only on successful completion and has no spent partial-progress account, so this cancellation consumes and refunds nothing. A refit completed in a prior publication remains paid and installed. A new attack cannot select a refitting own fleet as its attacker. No interruption grants healing, replacement equipment or a second colony completion; the cancelled slot stays idle until a new authorized planning command.

## ECF-008 Blockade pressure

Blockade is a finite standing fleet order against one contacted wartime empire at the fleet's current star. Require a currently observed colony of that empire there, a stationary own all-armed fleet, at least one operational (>50% hull) ship with cohesion>40, and no active battle, repair, refit, transit or assembly commitment. Unarmed traffic cannot blockade. The target is the **empire's colonies in that star**, not a private trade hub. A shared-system third empire is unaffected.

No hostile armed vessel of the target empire may be present for an **effective** blockade. Any stationary surviving armed hull contests it, including an ungrouped, damaged, repairing/refitting or sentry-engaged hull; hidden foreign intent is irrelevant. Show Contested with only legitimately observed hull facts, not their orders. Civilian hulls never contest it. The blockader must explicitly attack a defender if it wants to clear the star; arrivals do not begin combat. If a blockading fleet becomes a defending battle participant, clear its blockade order into combat; it must give a fresh Blockade command after combat to resume. Multiple own blockade fleets never stack.

A newly accepted blockade becomes active in the next publication and has no retroactive economic effect. At every economy boundary, freeze the existing order, war status, present qualifying blockaders, target colonies and contesting hulls. For each affected colony reduce **ordinary positive net production income by50%**, rounding down the retained income, after workforce/support penalties and before neutral-party trade income. Example: ordinary net701 internal becomes350, then an unrelated live trade +100 gives450. Research, support, population growth, stored bank/earmarks and completed buildings are unaffected. No starvation or population loss is introduced. Existing reserve/assembly/refit rules spend the resulting income normally; banked production can still finish projects. A production blockade neither disables founding nor grants settlement rights.

Example: order issued T, active at T+1; first reduced interval T+1->T+2. An enemy reinforcement arrives during that interval: its arrival does not change the frozen income, but publication T+2 shows Contested and T+2->T+3 is unreduced. A blockader destroyed/leaving in that interval similarly stops pressure from the next snapshot. Accepted peace is the explicit exception: it suppresses blockade reductions before that boundary's economy. Manual Lift blockade accepted in planning releases the order before that boundary's economy; its own preview gives the immediate date. Movement, voluntary repair/refit or disbanding the blockading force also lifts its order and requires confirmation of lost pressure. Blockade is never a hidden modifier inferred from mere warship presence.

The blockader sees the target empire/star and Effective/Contested/Inactive, not output, bank, project delay, private hubs or target population. The owner sees a named Blockade production reduction in colony breakdowns, current and conditional forecasts, queues, refits and assembly estimates. Pending enemy moves/peace answers are never represented as certain. A known current blockade uses the same pure economy function in all own forecasts and actual resolution; own hypothetical lifting must not change foreign facts or tokens.

## ECF-009 Deterministic rival military policy

Introduce a recognized `limited_conflict_v1` policy for new-rules rivals alongside their existing expansion and cooperation policies. No omniscient strategic score, hidden resource queries, injected hulls, free payments, RNG draw, difficulty advantage or species-ID exception. Decisions consume only that empire's immutable projected view and issue the normal commands; refresh only its own private revision between dependent accepted commands. Preserve old rules' exact planner behavior.

**Scheduling:** resolve own emergency/peace responses first, evaluate war-declaration eligibility next, then reserve an otherwise idle military production slot and issue military orders, then run ordinary diplomacy/expansion/development for remaining authority. A war-eligible AI does not emit a fresh ordinary accord offer in that same turn before its declaration decision. Existing pending offers and agreements still block its autonomous declaration. Military slot selection prevents the expansion planner claiming that same idle slot; it never overrides an already active project, human authority or finite assembly reservation. All planners use frozen legitimate foreign facts and updated own accepted authority.

**Construction and grouping:** peaceful target2 escorts, wartime target4, never deliberately exceed6 existing/committed armed hulls. Before the first second colony exists, target0 in peace; war permits defense immediately. Use at most one military-producing colony per empire, the lowest ColonyId eligible owned colony with positive ordinary production and an idle production slot. Never cancel an active project, spend earmarks or repeatedly preempt a settlement/refit/assembly contribution. Once a military project begins, let it finish; at a finite completed/idle slot reevaluate its target. Other colonies retain expansion/development. If target is met, release military allocation. Choose the cheapest available known legal armed design, then highest attack, armor, speed and lowest design ID, with ordinary build costs and existing equipment rules. No invented missing-role fallback: without any legal armed design, report policy cannot arm and pursue peace. Group eligible co-located warships into at most two fleets of up to4 each, in stable VesselId order, using existing normal fleet commands and available fleet slots; no free officer commission or silent officer transfer. Ungrouped warships can defend until a group is available.

**Readiness and priorities:** healthy means hull>=75% and cohesion>=60, with eligible stationary condition for the proposed action. Prefer return/repair for any own military force with hull<75% or cohesion<60; preserve existing emergency response priorities. Then relieve own known effective blockades, protect owned colony stars with locally observed enemy warships, attack a co-located uncommitted hostile force, and finally move a spare healthy force toward the closest known hostile colony system by integer distance/StarId. Last-observed colonies permit investigation travel only; arrival/current observation is required for Attack/Blockade. Keep the lowest-ID healthy fleet at an owned colony system if another force can act; do not abandon the sole defensive force for a remote blockade. Never infer a hidden home or pursue a disappeared ship. No sentry attack policy is added here.

For voluntary attack require at least2 armed ships and current own effective total firepower >= observed opposing effective total firepower, and own total hull >= observed opposing total hull. Equality qualifies; this is an advisory local rule, not an omniscient battle prediction. Use Cautious, Keep together and nearest known eligible own return system by distance/StarId, Repair on arrival. A force without a legal return port does not initiate an attack. Defenders return fire using Cautious and withdraw under the ordinary thresholds. A healthy unopposed spare force blockades a currently observed enemy colony after arrival; it does not immediately attack and blockade in one turn. Bound military actions to one production selection, two fleet grouping operations and one standing-order/movement/combat command per own military fleet per planning turn; accepted actions follow the priorities above.

**Initiating war:** the AI is cautious, not permanently peaceful. Require five completed boundaries since first contact, no war/truce, no active agreement or pending ordinary offer with that opponent, directed opinion<=0, at least two healthy armed hulls in one eligible fleet and an observed territorial grievance. The grievance is at least one currently observed foreign colony sharing a star with an owned colony, with the shared claim continuously observed for five completed boundaries. Reset this claim timer when current observation/own claim ceases; no inference from hidden colonization or ship movement. Choose the lowest qualifying contacted EmpireId. A publicly displayed policy explanation lists these conditions but does not reveal which hidden military threshold the AI currently meets. It may first offer a settlement accord under its existing policy; a live agreement prevents autonomous declaration. Humans may deliberately declare despite an agreement and accept the breach consequences.

**Peace:** AI accepts every valid unconditional peace offer; it cannot gain land in this slice and prefers a ceasefire over indefinite attrition. It proposes peace after five completed war boundaries if it has no healthy armed force, if any owned colony is effectively blockaded, or if ten completed boundaries have passed without either side's battle/blockade activity visible to it. Otherwise it proposes after15 completed war boundaries. Use at most one peace proposal/response per opponent/turn and wait5 completed boundaries after its peace offer is declined/expired/withdrawn before another outgoing proposal. These limits never block responding to a human peace offer. Explain this conservative policy in player-facing help; it supplies an off-ramp, not a claim of sophisticated diplomacy or balanced challenge. Independent ordinary agreement AI remains suppressed for that wartime pair.

## ECF-012 Player experience

Diplomacy contact rows show Peace / War / Truce until turn, ordinary attitude reasons, own pending declaration or peace response, and agreement consequences. At Peace the war action is separated from routine proposals and opens a dated confirmation. At War show unconditional peace terms and the truce length; no barter grid or unexplained victory percentage. Update the older “empires compete peacefully” introduction for rules12 only. Solo explains the absence of another empire.

Fleet detail shows Armed wartime target, defensive doctrine/return/local fallback, observed enemy force selection, forecast, Attack, Withdraw and Blockade/Lift.
